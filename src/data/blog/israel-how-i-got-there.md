My first night in Israel, I slept at the home of my father's friend's son.

I didn't know the country. I didn't speak the language.

I remember two thoughts from that night. The first was: _this is the start._ The second was: _I really don't know where my road goes anymore._

## How I got there

I went to elementary, middle, and high school in Korea.

In Korea, one exam decides a lot. The 수능 (Suneung), the national college entrance exam, sorts you into universities. For many people it feels like it sorts your life too.

I did badly on it.

The normal path after that is to study for another year and take it again. That was the path everyone around me expected.

My father was the one who told me there was another option: studying in Israel.

I had no plan beyond "this is possible." I didn't know anyone there. I didn't speak Hebrew.

I went anyway. I left at Christmas.

## What everyone said

My friends' reactions were about what you'd expect.

"Are you going to go dodge missiles?"

"Why would you do that? Just take the exam a third time and get into a good university in Korea."

From the outside, it looked like running away from the normal path. And they had a point about one thing. I didn't know what I was walking into.

What I learned quickly is that going to Israel is easy. You just get on a plane.

Staying is the hard part.

## Learning to live in Hebrew

At the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, classes are taught in Hebrew. I couldn't start a degree with the Hebrew I had, which was none.

So I started from the bottom.

First I did **Ulpan**, an intensive Hebrew language program. Then I did **Mechina**, a preparatory program before university. That's where I learned enough Hebrew to sit in a lecture hall.

I expected that year to be the part I had to survive.

It wasn't. That first year was fun.

Maybe it's my personality. I had arrived with no language and no connections, and somehow I enjoyed it.

## The real test

Then I started my first year at Hebrew University, studying Computer Science and Mathematics. In Hebrew.

That was when it got really hard.

Looking back, I didn't know my story would start this way. I didn't plan to study abroad. I didn't plan on Israel. A bad exam result and one suggestion from my father put me on a plane.

But I think the line I learned that first year is still true for me: getting somewhere is easy. Staying, and getting through it, is the hard part.

That first year at university is where the staying really started. It's the next story.
